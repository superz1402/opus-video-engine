# TREATMENT — "WOH LADY JO NVIDIA KE ANDAR THI" · 2-minute style demo
**Style:** Urban Sketch · Pen & Wash (lemo-opuscar `styles/urban-sketch`) · **Format:** 1920×1080, 24 fps (figures boil at 12 fps), ≈120 s, Hinglish voice (hi-IN-SwaraNeural, +12%), handwritten margin captions, −14 LUFS.

This is a **style test**: the film's real opening (script lines L1–L6, verbatim) shot entirely as a location sketchbook. Same content as the two sibling demos (Crayon Picture Book, 1930s Rubber Hose) so Bilal judges style only.

## Benchmark
- **Learn:** the urban-sketchers' grammar (line first, wash second, colour off-register, the page edge and margins as part of the picture; handwriting as the only type) and *Paperman*'s one-coloured-object rule — here the pink dress carries the whole film's colour emphasis.
- **Don't take:** any demo subject (park, hat, wind), any character, melody or layout from the references.

## Three candidate structures
1. **One page, one night (chosen).** A single sketchbook spread drawn "on location" through L1–L3; a page turn at "dedh saal baad" opens a second spread where the *same scene* is re-sketched colder as evidence (L4); the last spread is the reportage desk — the table and the four khane drawn as panels (L5–L6). The page turn is the film's one native move, landing exactly on the story's time jump.
2. **Travel diary of days.** A page per beat, six small pages. Rejected: fragments the one-night scene; the evidence turn needs the *same* drawing seen twice, which separate pages weaken.
3. **Colour-chase.** The pink wash travels across one long page like a protagonist (Red Balloon grammar). Rejected: makes the dress a gimmick; the story is about evidence, not the dress — the dress is the colour *event*, not the hero.

**Chosen: 1.** Logline: *A reporter sketches a rainy Taipei dinner as it happens; a year and a half later the same sketch is re-drawn as evidence — and the page itself becomes the investigation table.* Arc: sketch (warm, alive) → re-sketch (cold, annotated) → the desk (four empty khane waiting to be filled). Ending echoes the opening: the final pull-back shows the whole first page again, now with its margin full of handwriting.

## Beat sheet (locks to voice; music grid 3/4 waltz, bar = 3 beats)

## Subtitle & title design
All text is handwriting on the page (Caveat hand, sepia ink). Voice captions are handwritten lines in the lower margin of the page, written on as spoken, held ≥ max(1.8 s, speech + 0.6 s) — never an overlay box. Corner note (day · place · weather) top-left of spread 1; annotations in spread 2 margins; title handwritten in the final pull-back's bottom margin.

## Honesty notes
Figures are blank-faced ink sketches (the style's own reserve rule): no real faces, no real photos, no logos. Every spread is a fresh sketch — S4 re-draws the scene colder rather than recolouring S1's drawing.
