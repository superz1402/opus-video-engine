# TREATMENT — "WOH LADY JO NVIDIA KE ANDAR THI" · 2-minute style demo
## Style: 1930s Rubber Hose Cartoon (`styles/rubber-hose`) · Content: the film's opening (narration L1–L6, verbatim)

## 1. Candidate structures
1. **Proscenium revue** — one stage set; the set redresses itself between numbers (lane → dining room → doorway → desk). Simple, but the redress gags eat the narration's time.
2. **The Photo Is the Hero** *(chosen)* — the famous dinner photograph is a framed picture on a wall. The camera pushes INTO the photo and it comes alive; at "dedh saal baad" the band stops, the photo freezes mid-gesture, is lifted off the wall, carried to an investigator's desk and dealt around with evidence cards; the film ends with the photo pinned centre of an evidence board between four doors. The through-line object carries every transition, and the ending literally restages the opening — now annotated. It also serves the film's thesis: a photo is not a verdict.
3. **Bouncing-ball singalong** — narration phrases on cards with a hopping ball. Charming, but it turns an investigation into a jingle and fights the content's gravity.

**Why 2:** the opening narration itself is about a photo ("Is photo ko dhyaan se dekho"); making the photo the hero is the structure the content asks for, and its native moves (film-as-object, iris, transformation) all serve it. It shares nothing with the library demo's chase structure, opening, signature shot or ending.

## 2. Logline & arc
A framed photograph of a rainy-night dinner comes alive to a hot-jazz band — a lady, a CEO, a kiss, a car — until, eighteen months later, the music stops, the picture freezes, and it is carried to a desk to become Evidence No. 1: setup (a charming dinner) → turn (the same image, frozen and filed) → ending (four doors open: what is recorded, alleged, denied, unknown — the audience will judge).

## 3. Benchmark (grammar only)
- Early Fleischer shorts (c. 1930–33): the whole world breathes/dances; surreal object life; ink-and-frame devices. **Learn:** breathing sets, mickey-mousing, the iris as a toy. **Don't take:** any character, design, melody or shot.
- *Cuphead* (modern finish): how clean the B&W print, type and big-band score can be. **Learn:** contrast discipline, title lettering weight. **Don't take:** designs, tunes, bosses, logos.

## 4. Cast (all original designs, no likenesses, no existing cartoon IP)
- **THE PHOTO** — a framed picture with a brass plate; inside it, the world moves. The hero.
- **MR. JACKET** — a round, potato-bodied fellow in a dark jacket; pie eyes, white gloves, huge shoes. A generic big boss, not a portrait.
- **MISS ROSE** — a pear-shaped lady in a pale dress (reads white in B&W) with a floppy hat and a handbag that has its own opinions.
- **THE DAUGHTER** — a small round girl with a bow, seated at the table.
- **THE CAR** — an object character: headlight eyes, grille grin, honks on the beat.
- **THE CLOCK** — a wall clock whose hands spin for "dedh saal baad"; its pendulum is a leg that kicks.
- **REPORTERS** — three little fellows with box cameras (flash-bulb pops on the beat) and notebooks.
- **EVIDENCE CARDS & FOUR DOORS** — dealt cards; doors lettered RECORD · AAROP · INKAAR · AGYAAT.

## 6. Cue map (tempo grid: 120 BPM — one beat = 12 frames at 24 fps; characters on twos, camera on ones)
Exact section times lock to the real Swara word timings (voice first, picture follows). Sections:
- **A · Title & lane (L1):** ragtime vamp, rain bed; title letters hop on beats; push-in lands on "sheeshe ke peeche".
- **B · Dinner (L2a):** warm swing, clarinet lead; whisper on a held glance-chain (woodblock ticks); kiss = cymbal kiss + heart-puff on the beat.
- **C · Doorway (L2b–L3):** brighter 2-beat oom-pah; each rice packet hand-off = one note; car honk = two trumpet blasts; newspaper shade rolls down on a snare roll.
- **D · Dedh saal baad (L4):** full **band stop** at "Dedh saal baad" — freeze, projector clatter only; clock ticks accelerate; a low tuba resumes under the allegation sentence; stamp thump on "pehla saboot".
- **E · The desk (L5):** xylophone + brushes; one card dealt per beat on "ek-ek karke"; magnifier pass on "ek hi ghadi par".
- **F · Four doors (L6):** theme restated, bigger band; one door per phrase-beat; final hit + iris out on "neev hain".
