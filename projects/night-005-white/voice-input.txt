What if you're driving home at 2 a.m.… and a woman in a white dress is standing in the middle of the road? You brake. You blink. She's gone. Then you look in your rear-view mirror — and she's sitting in your back seat.

The Woman in White. Every country has her. A bride's dress, a face you can never quite see. She walks empty roads and riverbanks — always at night, always alone, always looking for something she lost.

Chicago, the 1930s. Drivers kept picking up a young woman in a white gown on Archer Avenue. She'd ask to be taken home — then vanish outside Resurrection Cemetery. Resurrection Mary. Mexico tells it older: La Llorona, the wailing woman by the river, searching for her children.

Witnesses agree on one thing: she isn't chasing you. She's grieving. And grief that old doesn't see the living — it sees what it lost. So learn the rule before you need it. If she gets in, don't speak. And whatever you do — don't look in the mirror.

So tell me. Is she real? If she is — whose road is she walking tonight? And if she stood in front of your car right now… would you stop? …Don't look in the mirror. Night six: the woman who asks you one question.
