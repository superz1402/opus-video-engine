What if you're walking home alone at night… and under the last streetlight, a woman is waiting? A long coat. Long hair. A surgical mask. She steps toward you and asks, very politely — "Am I pretty?"

Kuchisake-onna. The slit-mouthed woman. Japan's most feared face — because you never see all of it. The mask is the point. She chooses quiet streets, school routes, the last walk home. And she always asks the same question first.

In 1979, the story exploded. Children across Japan swore she was waiting on their street. Schools sent kids home in groups. Police patrolled. Newspapers printed everything. But the tale is far older — Edo-period stories already told of a woman with a torn mouth, asking strangers if she was beautiful.

Here's why the question is a trap. Say no — and she attacks. Say yes — and she takes off the mask. Underneath: a smile cut from ear to ear. "How about now?" she asks. The survivors' rules: never give a clean answer. Say "you're average." Keep it vague. Or throw hard candies at her feet — she has to stop and pick them up. That's your window. Run.

So tell me. Is she real? If she is — which street is she waiting on tonight? And if she stepped into your light right now and asked… what would you say? …Answer carefully. Night seven: the winged man who appeared before everything went wrong.
